## SECTION 8: FAQ

**Headline:** Frequently Asked Questions

**1. I'm not flexible at all. Can I still do yoga?**
Absolutely! Most of our students start with limited flexibility — that's exactly why they come to us. Our instructors specialize in working with beginners and will modify every pose for your current range of motion. You'll start where you are and progress safely at your own pace.

**2. How long does it take to see flexibility improvements?**
Most students notice improvements within 2-4 weeks of consistent practice (2-3 sessions per week). However, everyone's body is different. Your instructor will track your progress and adjust your practice to help you reach your flexibility goals as efficiently and safely as possible.

**3. I sit at a desk all day. Will yoga help my stiffness?**
Yes! Desk workers are one of our most common student profiles. Our instructors create targeted routines for tight hips, hamstrings, shoulders, and lower back — all common problem areas from prolonged sitting. Many students report reduced stiffness and better posture within the first few weeks.

**4. Can yoga help prevent injuries at the gym?**
Yes! Many of our students use yoga to improve their gym performance and prevent injuries. Increased flexibility improves your range of motion for exercises like squats and deadlifts, while yoga's focus on proper alignment and core strength reduces injury risk. Several students report improvements in their gym training after adding yoga to their routine.

**5. How often should I practice yoga for flexibility?**
We recommend 2-3 sessions per week for consistent flexibility gains. Your instructor will design a schedule based on your goals and availability. The key is consistency — regular practice yields better results than sporadic intense sessions.

**6. I have tight hips/hamstrings. Can yoga help?**
Absolutely! Tight hips and hamstrings are among the most common issues we address. Our instructors use targeted stretches and poses to safely increase hip and hamstring flexibility over time. Many students who couldn't touch their toes can do so within 6-8 weeks of consistent practice.
