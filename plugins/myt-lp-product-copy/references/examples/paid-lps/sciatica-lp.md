## SECTION 8: FAQ

**Headline:** Frequently Asked Questions
*(Select 5–6 of the 7 options below for the final page)*

**1. Is yoga actually good for sciatica?**
Yes — and there's strong evidence to support it. Yoga addresses several of the root causes that keep sciatica pain active: tight hips and hamstrings that pull on the lower back, weak core muscles that put excess load on the spine, and poor posture from prolonged sitting. Done correctly and at the right pace, yoga is one of the most effective non-invasive tools for managing and reducing sciatica pain.

**2. I've never done yoga before. Can I start if I have sciatica?**
Absolutely — and a 1-on-1 format is actually ideal for beginners with sciatica. Your instructor starts with the gentlest, most accessible poses and progressions, explains everything clearly, and makes sure you're never in a position that risks aggravating your pain. Many of our students had never done yoga before and found it far easier to start safely with live guidance than with any video.

**3. How quickly can I expect to feel relief?**
Most students notice a meaningful reduction in pain within 2–4 weeks of consistent practice (2–3 sessions per week). That said, everyone experiences sciatica differently — pain duration, your overall movement patterns, and consistency all play a role. Your instructor tracks your progress and adjusts the plan, so you're moving toward relief as efficiently as possible.

**4. Can yoga make sciatica worse?**
It can — but only if the wrong poses are done without proper guidance. Some forward folds, deep twists, and high-intensity backbends can aggravate the nerve if your body isn't ready for them. This is precisely why YouTube videos and group classes can be risky for sciatica. With a 1-on-1 instructor who knows your history, every session is designed to relieve — not trigger — your pain. Your instructor will flag anything to avoid based on your specific situation.

**5. I can't easily get on and off the floor. Can I still do yoga for sciatica?**
Yes. Many effective sciatica relief poses can be done seated in a chair or standing, with no floor work required. Your instructor will design your practice around your current mobility — whether that means starting entirely chair-based or using props to make floor poses accessible. You'll work with your body exactly as it is right now.

**6 (Alternate). What kind of yoga is best for sciatica?**
Gentle, restorative, and Hatha yoga styles tend to work best for sciatica relief — the emphasis is on slow, controlled stretching, breath awareness, and building stability rather than intense movement. Your instructor will draw from these styles and select specific poses targeted at hip opening, lower back release, and core engagement. The style matters less than the personalization — what's most important is that each session is tailored to your pain and recovery stage.

**7 (Alternate). Can I do yoga during an active sciatica flare-up?**
In most cases, yes — but with significant modifications. During a flare-up, your instructor will shift to the gentlest possible movements: breathwork, light supine stretches, and poses that reduce nerve tension without loading the spine. The goal during a flare-up is to calm, not challenge. Once the acute pain subsides, your practice can progress again. Your instructor will guide you through both phases safely.
